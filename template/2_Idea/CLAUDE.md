# 2_Idea

The idea, from plain words to formal. **These are the researcher's claims: write here
only when asked.** When you do, keep their structure and voice, and do not soften a claim
into a hedge.

| File | Holds |
| ---- | ----- |
| `High_level.md` | the idea in plain words: topic, motivation, anchor papers, keywords. `/lit-search` starts from it |
| `Outline.md` | the researcher's plan: intermediate goals, and the output each should produce. Any shape |
| `Setup_and_Method.md` | the idea made formal: notation, problem, method, hypotheses. Headings are a suggestion |
| `Experiments.md` | what each experiment is for, under its E-id |

A useful test, not a rule: if something could be chosen differently without changing the
idea, it is probably a spec and fits better in `3_Implementation/`.

`5_Paper/preamble.tex` mirrors the notation here; when a symbol changes, say so.

## The outline

`Outline.md` is the researcher's plan, most useful while there is no set scheme yet. It is
for them first; for you it is context. It lives alongside `Experiments.md`, not before it:
a few early experiments may be planned while the rest is still an outline, and their
outcome may change the idea and the outline with it. Asked to refine or evaluate it, do so
in chat: challenge a vague goal, or one with no output that would show it is reached. It
has no fixed structure: do not impose one.

`High_level.md` may hold a rougher outline of its own, or none. When `Outline.md` is
empty, use that one; when the two disagree, say so and ask which holds.

## Handwritten notes

`Human_encoding/` holds the researcher's scanned notes on the idea, organised from
`Human_encoding/Human_encoding.md`, with notes in `Human_encoding/Notes/` that embed the
scans. Read a note when the researcher points you to it. Asked, you may create a note or
add embeds for scans they name.
